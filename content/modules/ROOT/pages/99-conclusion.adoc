= Conclusion
:navtitle: Conclusion

Congratulations — you have completed the Leapp-O-Matic lab and automated a RHEL 9 → RHEL 10 upgrade end to end with Ansible Automation Platform.

== What You Learned

* *Automated, validated upgrades.* You used the `role-rhel10-upgrader` role, driven through an AAP workflow, to upgrade RHEL 9 systems to RHEL 10 with LEAPP — no manual, per-host steps required.
* *Preflight is the safety net.* You saw a workflow *fail safely* on `node3` (insufficient memory) and `node4` (RHEL 8, unsupported OS). The role caught both *before* any destructive change, protecting those systems from a half-completed upgrade.
* *Version normalization.* `node1` (RHEL 9.5) and `node2` (RHEL 9.7) upgraded successfully without manual version alignment, because the role normalizes any RHEL 9.x host to RHEL 9.6 during Phase 1.
* *Secure credential handling.* You configured RHSM credentials in AAP and injected them at runtime instead of hardcoding secrets in playbooks.
* *Post-upgrade verification.* You verified the upgraded nodes (OS version, kernel, enabled repositories) and reviewed post-upgrade tasks such as `.rpmnew`/`.rpmsave` configuration conflicts.

== Next Steps

* Plan a production upgrade strategy for your own environment, applying the "fail safe, not fail late" principle.
* Review the LEAPP pre-upgrade report format so you can interpret and act on reported incompatibilities.
* Extend the workflow to fit your fleet — for example, adding notifications, approval gates, or scheduling for maintenance windows.
* Integrate Red Hat Insights to continuously track upgrade readiness across your systems.
